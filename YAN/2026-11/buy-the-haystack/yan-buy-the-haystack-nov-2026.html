Buy the Haystack: What Nvidia's Turbulent Month Can Teach Us About Staying the Course
 

Unless you've been completely unplugged this month, you've probably heard that Nvidia, one of the most celebrated companies of the AI era, has had a very public fall from grace.

In a matter of days, its co-founder and long-time CEO, Jensen Huang, resigned after a personal scandal. A company worth trillions of dollars lost hundreds of billions in value, and for a while it became one of the most doubted stocks in the market.

Then something interesting happened.

Nvidia reported its latest quarterly results, and the business looked remarkably strong. Revenue roughly doubled from a year ago, and the company raised its outlook for the year ahead.

The market wasn't impressed. The stock rose briefly, then gave back those gains and more in the days that followed.

Did anyone predict this? Could anyone have imagined, even a month ago, that the face of the AI boom would no longer be leading the company he built?

The answer is a definite no.

The Things We Can't Plan For

On September 10th, 2001, no one could have predicted that the world would change forever the next morning.

In January 2020, most of us had never heard the word COVID.

Watershed moments like these can crush a single company, and with it the retirement plans of anyone who had too much riding on it.

The biggest risk to your retirement isn't what we can plan for. It's everything we have no idea is coming.

Buy the Haystack, Not the Needle

This is why my investment philosophy has always been simple: don't try to find the needle in the haystack. Buy the haystack.

Remember Nortel? BlackBerry? Lehman Brothers? Each was a powerhouse in its day, and two of them went bankrupt. Thousands of employees had their nest eggs tied up in companies they believed in. And why wouldn't they?

Nvidia's story isn't over, and this isn't a prediction about where its stock goes next. That's exactly the point. Nobody knows. Not the analysts, not the headlines, and not me.

What I do know is this: with a broadly diversified portfolio, you'll own some losers. You'll also own the winners. And you don't need to know in advance which is which.

Good News Doesn't Guarantee a Smooth Ride

Here's the part of this story I find most valuable.

Nvidia did exactly what investors usually hope a company will do: it grew. The stock fell anyway. Our Investment Strategy team at IG pointed out that, after the sell-off, Nvidia was priced at some of its cheapest levels in more than a decade compared to its expected earnings.

Markets don't only react to what happens. They react to what was expected, to emotion, and to uncertainty about what comes next. In the short term, that can feel completely disconnected from reality.

That's why timing the market is so hard. You don't just need to be right about the news. You need to be right about how everyone else will react to it.

Where the Real Value Lies

When markets fall, the urge to do something is completely natural. It's human. Watching an investment drop 20, 30 or 50 per cent can feel like a gut punch.

But in 14 years as a CFP, I've learned that the most costly decisions rarely come from the markets themselves. They come from how we react to them: selling after a decline, chasing what has already gone up, or letting one headline undo years of good planning.

This is where a financial plan earns its keep. When your plan already accounts for how much risk you can reasonably take, when you'll need your money and where your income will come from, a market correction becomes something we prepared for, not something we react to.

It's also where good advice matters most. Sometimes the most valuable thing I can do for a client isn't finding the next great investment. It's being a steady voice and offering perspective when the headlines are at their loudest.

Simple, But Not Easy

Owning the whole haystack means I don't have to know what happens next week, next month or next year. And neither do you.

Building wealth can be simple, but it isn't easy. Ignore the noise, stick with your plan, and let time and discipline do the heavy lifting.

Life will go on. And you get to spend your energy on what really matters: living your Rich Life.
