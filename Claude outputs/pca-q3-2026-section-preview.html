Build, partner, buy, finance or sell? Your next stage of growth

Tariffs have pushed many Canadian business owners to focus on protecting margins, customers and cash flow. That matters, but defence shouldn’t be the whole strategy. The Q3 2026 newsletter from IG Private Company Advisory looks at where a business’s next stage of growth could come from, and who is best positioned to pursue it.

The article walks through the trade-offs of building, partnering or acquiring, how growth can sometimes be financed with debt rather than by selling equity, and why a sale can be worth evaluating even when a company is performing well.

The takeaway: before committing to the next stage, find out what your business can prudently borrow and what a credible buyer might pay. Those two numbers can change the decision. If you own a business and would like to talk through your options, we’re happy to help.

Deal flow holds its ground

Canadian deal activity in Q3 2026 landed at 64 transactions (35 M&A, 29 private placements), compared with 85 in Q2. Private placements held at 29, matching Q3 2025. Activity eased from a stronger second quarter, with M&A and private placements both lower.
 

Tech and materials lead in volume, energy leads in value

Over the trailing 12 months, IT and materials dominated Canadian deal volume — 77 and 73 transactions respectively, together claiming nearly half of all activity. In contrast, deal value tells a different story. Energy generated roughly $66 billion (CAD), much of which reflects a handful of large transactions, the five largest account for nearly 90%, while materials contributed nearly $60 billion. Industrials (41 deals) and health care (35) rounded out the busy end of the quarter.

The lesson for owners: your sector’s deal count doesn’t necessarily predict its dollar weight. Sector totals are driven by a few large transactions and say little about any one company’s value.
